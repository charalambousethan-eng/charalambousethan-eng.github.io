Schofield Capsule

A watch box that is opened like a secret: twist, reveal, then crack the code.

Client
Schofield Watch Company
Brief
Giles Ellis
Type
Packaging, artefact
Status
Working prototype
01 · Brief

Ritual, secrecy and belonging

Schofield buyers collect stories as much as watches. The brief was a watch box built around ritual, secrecy and belonging, and that would not end up in the bin.

02 · Process

From box to capsule

Early ideas used trays, hinges and watch rolls. I dropped them because they felt like every other box. The capsule's rugged cylinder fitted Schofield's brutalist look and the nuclear theme.

The ends twist off on M39 and M35 threads. The body has a glass panel so the watch can be seen while it is sealed, and three M6 screws let an owner fix it inside a drawer.

03 · The hidden compartment

The nuclear biscuit

Behind the second end cap is a hidden compartment holding a watch cloth and a paperstone 'nuclear biscuit'. Snapping the biscuit reveals the code word for the Buoys Club, Schofield's owners' club.

04 · Outcome

A working prototype

The production design is CNC-machined 316L stainless steel with a PVD-coated nuclear plate. The prototype is 3D printed.
