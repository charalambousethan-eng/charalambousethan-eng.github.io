Schofield Capsule

A watch box that is opened like a secret: twist, reveal, then crack the code.

Client
Schofield Watch Company
Brief
Set by Giles Ellis, owner of Schofield Watch Company
Product
Watch box
Status
Prototype
01 · Brief

Ritual, secrecy and belonging

Schofield buyers collect stories as much as watches. Giles Ellis, owner of Schofield Watch Company, set the brief: a watch box built around ritual, secrecy and belonging, that would not end up in the bin.

02 · Process

From box to capsule

I sketched trays, hinged cases, barrel boxes and watch rolls first. They all worked, but they felt like every other watch box. The capsule's rugged cylinder fitted Schofield's brutalist look and opened the door to a nuclear theme.

03 · The hidden compartment

The nuclear biscuit

Giles runs a secret owners' club, the Buoys Club. You get in with a password, and at the moment that password arrives in a letter Giles posts to every buyer of a Schofield watch.

I saw that as a problem worth solving. Every letter costs Giles money and sits apart from the watch. The password could live inside the box instead.

So the capsule has a secret compartment holding a watch wipe and a "nuclear biscuit". The idea comes from nuclear launch codes: nobody is allowed to know the code in advance, so when the moment comes, the card is snapped open to reveal it. Here, snapping the biscuit reveals the password to the Buoys Club.

The biscuit completes the capsule's story and gives the owner something to do, not just something to read. It also removes the need for a separate letter, in a more considered and engaging way.

04 · Materials

Material selection

Stainless steel

Chosen for its durability and weight, which give a sense of treasure and security. It is 100% recyclable and suits the raw, brutalist look of the Schofield brand.

Cork

A renewable, carbon-positive material. It gives the watch a soft, non-abrasive nest and is naturally antimicrobial and long-lasting.

Curved glass

Lets the watch be seen while it stays protected. Glass is infinitely recyclable and avoids the cheap feel of plastic.

Paperstone

A heavy-duty composite made from 100% recycled paper and non-petroleum resins. It has the stone-like strength the biscuit needs for its snap, while staying eco-friendly.

05 · Prototype

The capsule, printed

I 3D printed the capsule to test its proportions, the twist-off end caps and how the watch sits behind the glass panel. The orange end cap carries the nuclear symbol that runs through the whole story.

06 · User experience

Opening the capsule

Opening the capsule is a short ritual. Twist the left end to reveal the watch, then turn the right end to find the watch cloth and the nuclear biscuit.
